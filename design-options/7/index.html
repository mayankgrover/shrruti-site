Her story

The pathologist who lives with MS

Dr. Shruti Grover is a pathologist with multiple sclerosis. She talks about it openly, and it shapes how she practises: careful about what she claims, and clear about what she cannot promise.

She manages her own MS with lifestyle, movement, yoga, meditation and acupuncture, alongside the care her own doctors advise. That is also how she works with you: on pain, fatigue, stress and sleep, in a plan you can actually keep.

  • Medicine first. MBBS at the University of Delhi (UCMS), then MD in Pathology at UCMS, then a Fellowship in Oncopathology at the Rajiv Gandhi Cancer Institute & Research Centre. A pathologist is trained to read the evidence before acting.
  • Support, never a cure. For people with MS she offers comfort, fatigue and quality-of-life support — and never a replacement for your neurologist.
What the research says about acupuncture and MS. The evidence note

What the published reviews actually say about acupuncture and multiple sclerosis — and what they do not say.

  • A 2014 review of 12 studies suggested possible improvement in quality of life, fatigue, spasticity and pain, but the poor study designs made firm conclusions difficult.
  • A 2021 review found that traditional Chinese acupuncture and scalp acupuncture appeared to help symptoms including fatigue, pain, walking difficulties and bladder problems.
  • A 2018 review found there was not enough evidence that acupuncture could reduce spasticity in MS.
  • Acupuncture is generally considered safe when it is done by an experienced, trained practitioner using sterile single-use needles.

So on this site, acupuncture for MS is written as support — comfort, fatigue and quality of life — and never as a cure, never as a replacement for your neurologist, and never as a promise about what your body will do.

Questions people ask

FAQ

What happens in the first consultation?

You talk through your symptoms, medical history and medicines, and what you want to be able to do again. If a treatment is appropriate, you may have it in the same visit. You leave with a written plan.

Is acupuncture safe, and what should I tell her first?

Acupuncture is generally considered safe when it is done by an experienced, trained practitioner using sterile single-use needles. Tell Dr. Grover about your medicines, any bleeding or clotting problem, pregnancy, or a pacemaker before your visit. She will go through this with you anyway.

Does acupuncture treat multiple sclerosis?

No. It is not a cure for MS and it does not replace disease-modifying treatment or your neurologist. Research on acupuncture in MS is limited and mixed, so Dr. Grover uses acupuncture for MS support — comfort and quality of life — alongside your own treatment. The evidence note on this page links to the source.
