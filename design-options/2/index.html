Her story

She treats the condition she lives with — carefully, and without pretending it is simple.

On the evidence. Acupuncture in MS is a complementary therapy. Reviews report possible benefit for symptoms such as fatigue, pain and walking difficulty, but the research is limited and study quality is mixed, so it is never presented as a cure or a replacement for disease-modifying treatment. Source: Multiple Sclerosis Trust, “Acupuncture” (A-Z of MS), mstrust.org.uk/a-z/acupuncture (read 13 November 2026).

Dr. Grover has multiple sclerosis. She manages it the way she works with patients: sleep, movement, yoga, meditation and acupuncture — alongside the care her own doctors advise.

Before integrative medicine she spent years in pathology: MBBS at the University of Delhi (UCMS), then MD Pathology, then a fellowship in oncopathology at the Rajiv Gandhi Cancer Institute & Research Centre. A pathologist looks at evidence before acting, and that habit shows in the clinic.

So the promise is a modest one. You get an assessment, a plan you can keep, and honest talk about what acupuncture can and cannot do. No cure claims, no guarantees — and no pressure to book a package of sessions.

FAQ

What happens in the first consultation?

You talk through your symptoms, medical history and medicines, and what you want to be able to do again. If a treatment is appropriate, you may have it in the same visit. You leave with a written plan.

Is acupuncture safe, and what should I tell her first?

Acupuncture is generally considered safe when it is done by an experienced, trained practitioner using sterile single-use needles. Tell Dr. Grover about your medicines, any bleeding or clotting problem, pregnancy, or a pacemaker before your visit. She will go through this with you anyway.

Does acupuncture treat multiple sclerosis?

No. It is not a cure for MS and it does not replace disease-modifying treatment or your neurologist. Research on acupuncture in MS is limited and mixed: a 2014 review of 12 studies suggested possible improvement in quality of life, fatigue, spasticity and pain, but the study designs were too weak to draw firm conclusions. Dr. Grover uses acupuncture for MS support — comfort and quality of life — alongside your own treatment.
