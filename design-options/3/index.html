Her story

A doctor who lives with MS

She treats the condition she lives with — with evidence, and without promises she cannot keep.

Dr. Grover has multiple sclerosis. She manages it the way she works with patients: sleep, movement, yoga, meditation and acupuncture, alongside the care her own doctors advise.

Before integrative medicine she trained in pathology — MBBS at the University of Delhi (UCMS), MD Pathology, then a fellowship in oncopathology at the Rajiv Gandhi Cancer Institute & Research Centre. A pathologist looks at the evidence before acting, and that habit shows in the clinic.

What she works with

Acupuncture is a complement to your medical care here, not a replacement for it — and never a cure.

  • ✓Traditional Chinese AcupunctureFine needles at specific points, with sterile single-use needles.
  • ✓Integrative medicineAssessment, treatment and lifestyle changes that fit your life.
  • ✓Lifestyle medicineSleep, movement, stress and daily habits.
  • ✓MS supportComplementary care for fatigue, pain and spasticity, alongside your neurologist.

Evidence note: acupuncture in MS is a complementary therapy. Reviews report possible benefit for symptoms such as fatigue, pain and walking difficulty, but the research is limited and study quality is mixed, so it is never presented as a cure or a replacement for disease-modifying treatment. Source: Multiple Sclerosis Trust, “Acupuncture” (A-Z of MS), mstrust.org.uk/a-z/acupuncture (read 13 November 2026).

Questions people ask

FAQ

What happens in the first consultation?

You talk through your symptoms, medical history and medicines, and what you want to be able to do again. If a treatment is appropriate, you may have it in the same visit. You leave with a written plan.

Is acupuncture safe, and what should I tell her first?

Acupuncture is generally considered safe when it is done by an experienced, trained practitioner using sterile single-use needles. Tell Dr. Grover about your medicines, any bleeding or clotting problem, pregnancy, or a pacemaker before your visit. She will go through this with you anyway.

Does acupuncture treat multiple sclerosis?

No. It is not a cure for MS and it does not replace disease-modifying treatment or your neurologist. Research on acupuncture in MS is limited and mixed: a 2014 review of 12 studies suggested possible improvement in quality of life, fatigue, spasticity and pain, but the study designs were too weak to draw firm conclusions. Dr. Grover uses acupuncture for MS support — comfort and quality of life — alongside your own treatment.
