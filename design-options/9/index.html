What the research says about acupuncture and MS. Acupuncture is a complementary therapy sometimes used by people with multiple sclerosis. A 2014 review of 12 studies suggested possible improvement in quality of life, fatigue, spasticity and pain, but the poor study designs made firm conclusions difficult. A 2021 review found traditional and scalp acupuncture appeared to help symptoms including fatigue, pain, walking difficulties and bladder problems. A 2018 review found there was not enough evidence that acupuncture could reduce spasticity in MS.

So on this site acupuncture for MS is written as support — comfort, fatigue and quality of life — and never as a cure or a replacement for your neurologist.

Source: Multiple Sclerosis Trust, “Acupuncture” (A-Z of MS), last updated 1 April 2022 — mstrust.org.uk/a-z/acupuncture (read 13 November 2026).

Before you write

Questions people ask.

What happens in the first consultation?

You talk through your symptoms, medical history and medicines, and what you want to be able to do again. If a treatment is appropriate, you may have it in the same visit. You leave with a written plan.

Is acupuncture safe, and what should I tell her first?

Acupuncture is generally considered safe when it is done by an appropriately trained practitioner using sterile, single-use needles; serious side effects are rare. Tell Dr. Grover before your visit about your medicines, any bleeding or clotting problem, pregnancy, or a pacemaker — these are the things a practitioner needs to know first. She will go through them with you anyway.

Sources: NCCIH, “Acupuncture: Effectiveness and Safety” (read 13 November 2026) — sterile single-use needles and the safety record; NHS, “A guide to acupuncture treatment” (read 13 November 2026) — what the practitioner needs to know before treatment.

Does acupuncture treat multiple sclerosis?

No. It is not a cure for MS and it does not replace disease-modifying treatment or your neurologist. Research on acupuncture in MS is limited and mixed, so Dr. Grover uses acupuncture for MS support — comfort and quality of life — alongside your own treatment. The evidence note on this page links to the source.
