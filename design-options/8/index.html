The evidence note

What the research says about acupuncture and MS

Acupuncture is a complementary therapy sometimes used by people with multiple sclerosis. A 2014 review of 12 studies suggested possible improvement in quality of life, fatigue, spasticity and pain, but the poor study designs made firm conclusions difficult. A 2021 review found traditional and scalp acupuncture appeared to help symptoms including fatigue, pain, walking difficulties and bladder problems. A 2018 review found there was not enough evidence that acupuncture could reduce spasticity in MS.

So on this site acupuncture for MS is written as support — comfort, fatigue and quality of life — and never as a cure or a replacement for your neurologist.

Sources: Multiple Sclerosis Trust, “Acupuncture” (A-Z of MS), last updated 1 April 2022, mstrust.org.uk/a-z/acupuncture (read 13 November 2026). For pain conditions, the US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health reports that research shows acupuncture may be helpful for back or neck pain and for knee pain linked to osteoarthritis, and that a 2017 American College of Physicians guideline listed acupuncture among the non-drug options recommended as first-line treatment for chronic low-back pain; for sciatica, reviews conclude it may be helpful but the quality of the research is not good enough for definite conclusions: nccih.nih.gov/health/acupuncture-effectiveness-and-safety (read 13 November 2026).

Questions people ask

FAQ

What happens in the first consultation?

You talk through your symptoms, medical history and medicines, and what you want to be able to do again. If a treatment is appropriate, you may have it in the same visit. You leave with a written plan.

Is acupuncture safe, and what should I tell her first?

Acupuncture is generally considered safe when it is done by an experienced, trained practitioner using sterile single-use needles. Tell Dr. Grover about your medicines, any bleeding or clotting problem, pregnancy, or a pacemaker before your visit. She will go through this with you anyway.

Does acupuncture treat multiple sclerosis?

No. It is not a cure for MS and it does not replace disease-modifying treatment or your neurologist. Research on acupuncture in MS is limited and mixed, so Dr. Grover uses acupuncture for MS support — comfort and quality of life — alongside your own treatment. The evidence note on this page links to the source.
